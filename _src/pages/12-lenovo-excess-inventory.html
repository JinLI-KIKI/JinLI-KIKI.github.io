The problem

When I took over the North American education Windows line, slow-moving SKUs and a mismatch between what had been planned and what was selling had left $12M of material committed to plans that no longer had demand. In planning terms this is MWD: material without demand.

MWD is expensive in two ways. It ties up cash, and every month it sits it costs warehousing and moves closer to obsolete. Once a plan is cancelled inside the supplier's liability window, the brand owns that material whether or not it was physically bought, so the clock is already running when you inherit it.

Five levers

No single action clears $12M. I used five, and the skill is knowing which one fits each part.

LeverWhat I didWhere it stops working
Extend the product's lifeRaised product change requests (PCRs) to push out end-of-life, so the existing material kept being used in builds.Only while the market still buys the model. The successor's launch sets a hard edge.
Move parts to the next generationGot components carried over onto successor models so they could be consumed there.Parts made for one model, such as its housing, rarely carry over.
Change the BOMReworked bills of materials so stranded parts could go into configurations that sell.Rework has a cost. It only pays when it beats writing the material off.
Commercial actionsWorked with sales to steer orders toward configurations that used the excess.Depends on what sales can sell, and sometimes needs price support.
Reallocate globallyMoved material to other regions that build or sell the same products.Region-specific parts, such as keyboards in a local layout, power cords and packaging, can only be used in their own region.

What it taught me

Every lever loses options with time

Extending life, migrating parts and reallocating all work best early. The closer a product gets to end-of-life, the fewer of them are left.

Claimed is not physical

The excess counted when a plan is cancelled and the material actually sitting in stock are different numbers. The real figure is only confirmed at stock-take.

Regional parts strand first

Common parts can usually find another home. Keyboards, power cords and packaging made for one region are what's left at the end.

It's a cross-team fix

Every lever needed someone else: R&D for PCRs and part changes, sales for demand, other regions for reallocation.
