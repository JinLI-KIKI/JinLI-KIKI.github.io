Lenovo case

China to Vietnam motherboard transition

To take North American supply out of China-origin tariffs, our ODM partner built new motherboard lines in Vietnam. I planned the months in which both sources ran side by side.

Why the board

For US customs, a notebook's country of origin generally follows where its motherboard is made. A notebook assembled in Vietnam around a China-made board still counts as China-made and still pays the China duty. So a move that only shifted final assembly would have changed nothing on the tariff bill. The move had to start at the board.

That made it a bigger change than moving an assembly line: new surface-mount lines, new process qualification, and a supply chain for components that now had to reach Vietnam instead of China.

Two sources at once

Start in parallel

The new Vietnam lines started alongside the China lines, which kept supplying while the new site qualified.

Ramp and burn down

As Vietnam capacity came up, more North American orders moved onto Vietnam boards, and China board stock was used up first.

Shift North America

North American supply moved to Vietnam boards as capacity allowed, cutting its tariff cost without a gap in supply.

What I did

  • Coordinated with the ODM partner as it set up new motherboard lines in Vietnam, running in sync with the existing China lines.
  • Planned dual-source supply: which North American demand stayed on China boards and which moved to Vietnam boards, week by week, as the new line ramped.
  • Used up China board inventory during the ramp, so the transition didn't leave boards to scrap.
  • Moved North American supply onto Vietnam boards as capacity allowed, lowering the tariff cost for the market while keeping supply continuous.

Why it can't be a switch

New lines ramp slowly

Capacity, yield and certification all lag at a new site. The old site has to keep covering the gap, and keeps paying the old duty while it does.

Rushing creates shortages

Move too fast and material runs short. Parts can be expedited, but on-time delivery isn't guaranteed; it depends on capacity, how practised the workers are and how well sourcing manages suppliers.

Move one region at a time

When one region moves and other regions keep building the same models in China, most material still has somewhere to go. Only the region-specific parts strand.

The chain gets longer

Many components still come from China, so they take longer to reach the new site. Plans have to be committed earlier, and that cost recurs every year.
