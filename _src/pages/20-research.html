Why UV-LEDs

Advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) use light to split an oxidant into short-lived radicals, mainly the hydroxyl radical, that attack contaminants which coagulation, filtration and chlorination leave behind: taste and odour compounds, pharmaceuticals, pesticides.

Mercury lamps have done this for decades. UV-LEDs are mercury-free, switch on instantly and can be built at the wavelength that suits each oxidant. What's missing is evidence of how they perform in real water, where carbonate, natural organic matter and bromide compete for the same radicals, winter water is cold, and every extra milligram of chlorine is a possible by-product. My PhD builds that evidence, from radical chemistry up to water taken from a treatment plant.

Methods I use

Bench-scale UV-LED irradiation
280 nm UV-LED setups with fluence determination, oxidant measurement and quenching protocols.
Radical probes
para-Chlorobenzoic acid (pCBA) to measure hydroxyl-radical exposure and Rct.
Organic matter
UV-Vis absorbance, DOC, fluorescence excitation-emission matrices (EEM) with PARAFAC and peak picking, LC-MS.
Disinfection by-products
THM and HAA formation potential under uniform formation conditions.
Chromatography
HPLC and GC for target compounds.
Modelling
Kinetic prediction from photolysis constants, Monte Carlo simulation and Sobol sensitivity analysis in R and Python.
