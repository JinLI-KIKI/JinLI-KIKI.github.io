The year

I joined Lenovo's global PC supply chain straight after my master's, as the planner responsible for North American notebooks. The day job was the monthly planning cycle: take demand, run supply planning against material and factory capacity, commit what could be built and sort out the gaps with the teams around me.

Three months in, the US election changed the year. Tariffs on China-made goods were signalled in November 2024 and arrived in February and March 2025, and most North American notebooks were built in China. Much of what I did after that was about getting ahead of them: shipping stock before the duty started, and moving motherboard supply to Vietnam so the products stopped counting as China-made.

Alongside that, I inherited an education notebook line with $12M of material that no longer had demand behind it, and I wrote the Python tool our team used to turn part-level commits into finished-unit commits. Each of these has its own page below.

Field notes

The terms these pages use, as I worked with them. Notebook supply chains have their own vocabulary, and most of the interesting decisions sit inside it.

IBP
Integrated business planning. The monthly cycle that lines demand, supply and finance up on one plan, so sales, operations and finance work from the same numbers.
Commit
What a factory or supplier promises to deliver, by week. Parts are committed part by part; the business needs commits for finished units, called boxes.
MWD
Material without demand. Parts bought or committed for plans that were later cut. Once a plan is cancelled inside the supplier's liability window, the brand owns that material, whether or not it was actually bought yet.
Region-specific parts
Keyboards in a local layout, power cords, packaging and labels. They can only be used in their own region, so they are the first parts to strand when a region's plan changes.
Origin follows the board
For US customs, a notebook's country of origin generally follows where its motherboard is made. Final assembly elsewhere does not change it.
Board build
Mounting components onto motherboards. It turns loose, reusable parts into a board tied to one model, which is why the timing of board builds matters so much when demand is uncertain.
ODM
Original design manufacturer. The partner factory that builds the notebooks. Its capacity follows the volume you load: under-load it and the capacity goes to someone else.
PCR
Product change request. The formal way to change a product, for example to extend its end-of-life date or change its bill of materials.

Where it went next

I left Lenovo in August 2025 to start my PhD in Tokyo, but I kept working on the problems from that year. My supply chain digital twin is an open model of a tariff-driven factory transfer for an anonymous laptop maker, built on synthetic data: stranded material, cutover timing, the longer chain, new-site ramp and factory footprint.
