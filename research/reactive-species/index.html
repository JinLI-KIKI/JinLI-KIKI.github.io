Reactive species in UV-LED oxidation

How many radicals do 280 nm UV-LEDs make from hydrogen peroxide and from chlorine, and what in real water takes them away?

When
2025 – now
Systems
UV-LED/H2O2 and UV-LED/chlorine at 280 nm
Methods
pCBA probe, Rct, Monte Carlo and Sobol sensitivity analysis
Status
Under reviewmanuscript, first author, 2026

The chemistry

Switch between the two oxidants. The chlorine system needs more careful accounting of which radical does the work.

The question

Plant operators choose an oxidant and a dose. What they actually buy is radical exposure: how much •OH (and, with chlorine, reactive chlorine species) the water sees per unit of UV dose. That number depends on the oxidant, the pH, the temperature and everything else dissolved in the water.

Most of what we know comes from mercury lamps at 254 nm. I measured it for 280 nm UV-LEDs, for both oxidants, across the conditions a drinking-water plant actually sees.

How I measured it

  1. Design the test matrix

    Oxidant dose, pH 5 to 9, temperature 4 to 20 °C, and matrix constituents: bicarbonate, humic acid and bromide.

  2. Probe the radicals

    Added para-chlorobenzoic acid (pCBA), a probe compound that reacts quickly with •OH, and tracked its decay to quantify hydroxyl-radical exposure and Rct, the exposure per unit of UV dose.

  3. Model the matrix

    Built a Monte Carlo and Sobol sensitivity workflow to rank which constituents control radical exposure when they vary together, as they do in real water.

  4. Check against real water

    Tested whether the lab results predict treatment of coagulated water from a full-scale plant, continued in the real-water project.

Early results

DoseOxidant dose was the main driver of performance in UV-LED/H2O2.
~36%slower with 5 mg/L humic acid; Rct fell steadily from 1 to 5 mg/L.
>30%slower with 0.1 mM bromide, which also raises a brominated by-product concern.
  • pH had a moderate effect (pH 5 > 7 > 9). Moving from 4 to 20 °C changed comparatively little.
  • Bicarbonate caused less than 6% inhibition over the range tested.
  • Compared with the closest earlier study at 265 nm, this work uses 280 nm, a wider range of oxidant doses and real coagulated water.

From work under review. Final values may change in the published paper.

Why it matters

If a plant knows which constituents eat its radicals, it can predict how an AOP will perform from routine water-quality data instead of a pilot study for every season. That is the bridge from this lab work to the real-water chapter of my PhD.
