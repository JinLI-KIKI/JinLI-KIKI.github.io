Real water at the dosing point

Lab water is clean and predictable. I take water from where an AOP would actually be dosed in a treatment plant, after coagulation, and measure what UV-LED treatment does to its organic matter and its potential to form disinfection by-products.

Water
Coagulated water from a full-scale plant, DOC about 1 mg/L
Systems
280 nm UV-LED alone, with H2O2 and with chlorine
Measured
DOC, UV254, fluorescence EEM, THM and HAA formation potential
Status
In progressPhD chapter, seasonal batches

Why this water

Most UV-LED oxidation studies on real water use raw source water. But an AOP in a drinking-water plant is usually dosed after coagulation, when much of the organic matter is already gone. What's left is less, different and harder to change, and it is what reacts with chlorine later to form trihalomethanes (THMs) and haloacetic acids (HAAs).

So the question is not just "does it remove things" but "does treating this water with UV-LED light and an oxidant raise or lower its potential to form by-products once it is chlorinated for distribution?"

The design

Each batch is collected at a different time of year, so the chapter covers seasonal changes in the water. Every experiment day includes its own untreated control.

Keeping it fair

With so little organic matter, the effects are small, so most of the work is making sure the comparison is honest.

Same chlorine at the start of the test

For the by-product test, every sample is chlorinated so the chlorine left after the fast reactions matches the untreated control. Leftover H2O2 consumes chlorine, so I size the extra dose from a side test.

Quench without distorting

Residual chlorine in optical samples is quenched with sulfite sized to what's left, because quenchers absorb UV themselves and would bias UV254.

Measure absorbance properly

At this low absorbance, a 1 cm cell's noise is as large as the effect, so UV254 is measured the same day in 5 cm quartz cells.

Replicates over extra points

By-product changes are small, so independent replicates matter more than more dose levels.

What I'm looking for

  • How much UV254, fluorescence and DOC change with each oxidant and dose, and how chlorine and H2O2 compare at the same UV dose.
  • Whether changes in fluorescence and UV absorbance actually track changes in THM and HAA formation potential. Destroying the parts of organic matter that absorb light is not the same as removing by-product precursors.
  • Whether the radical-exposure model from the reactive-species work predicts what happens in this water, and where chlorine demand from organic matter makes it drift.
