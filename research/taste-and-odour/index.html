Taste and odour in cold water

Geosmin and 2-MIB give tap water an earthy, musty taste at concentrations of a few nanograms per litre. I tested whether 280 nm UV-LEDs with free chlorine can remove them when the water is cold.

Compounds
Geosmin (GSM) and 2-methylisoborneol (2-MIB)
System
280 nm UV-LED with free chlorine (HClO)
Varied
Water temperature, pH and chlorine dose
Published
2026Desalination and Water Treatment, 326, 101746

The problem

Geosmin and 2-MIB are made by cyanobacteria and other microbes in reservoirs and rivers. They are harmless, but people can taste them at around 10 ng/L, and taste is how most customers judge their water. Coagulation, filtration and chlorination barely touch them.

Ozone works, but it becomes less efficient in cold water, and taste and odour problems do not stop in winter. A UV-based process that holds up at low temperature would give utilities another option.

What I did

  • Set up a 280 nm UV-LED/HClO advanced oxidation system for GSM and 2-MIB.
  • Measured degradation across water temperatures and pH values relevant to drinking-water treatment, at a range of chlorine doses and UV fluences.
  • After each run, measured temperature, pH, free chlorine and the remaining GSM and 2-MIB, to separate the effect of the oxidant from what was being used up.

Findings

<20%faster at most from more chlorine, even at 7 mg/L: dose has diminishing returns.
Cl2 runs outRates dipped under long irradiation as free chlorine was used up, before the UV dose was.
2 compoundsGSM and 2-MIB, the most common causes of earthy and musty taste complaints, tested side by side.

The practical message for a plant: adding chlorine beyond a modest dose buys little extra removal, and in long treatments the chlorine supply, not the light, becomes the limit.
